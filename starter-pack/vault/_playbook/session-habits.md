# Session habits — the three that make memory pay off

🟢 **active**

The hooks capture and recall on their own. These three habits are the part no hook can do for you,
and a vault without them fills up while getting less useful.

> [!tip] 1. Search before guessing
> When a command fails twice, or you think "have we hit this before", run `/second-brain pull <terms>`
> before trying a third fix. `pull` returns the full text of matching notes and journal entries, so
> the answer arrives as the actual command, not a title you then have to open.

> [!tip] 2. Consolidate on a rhythm
> `/second-brain consolidate` turns recent `Daily/` entries into curated notes. Nothing runs it for
> you. Recall ranks curated notes and ignores the journal, so an unconsolidated vault captures
> perfectly and retrieves nothing. Weekly is enough for most work.

> [!warning] 3. Publish before you call it done
> Substantial work leaves one curated note behind: what changed, why, and what to watch. Link it into
> its folder hub. A decision that exists only in a chat log is a decision you will make again.

## Why this order

Searching is the habit that pays back immediately, so it is the one that sticks. Consolidating is
what makes searching work later. Publishing is what makes the next person — usually you, months on —
able to trust what they find.
